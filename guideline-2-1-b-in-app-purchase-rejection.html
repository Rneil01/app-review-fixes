App Review Fixes

Guideline 2.1(b): "in-app purchase products have not been submitted"

This is the most common rejection for first-time subscription apps, and the fix is mostly in App Store Connect, not your code.

Apple's guideline 2.1(b) says in-app purchases must be "complete, up-to-date, visible to the reviewer and functional." Developers report rejection messages like these:

One or more of the In-App Purchase products referenced in your app have not been submitted for review.

The subscription screen did not display any available plans for purchase.

Why it happens

  • Your first subscription must ship with an app version. Apple reviews your first-ever in-app purchase only together with a binary. Submitting the build alone isn't enough.
  • The product isn't "Ready to Submit." The "In-App Purchases and Subscriptions" section only appears on the version page once at least one product reaches that status.
  • The link drops off after a rejection. After a rejection, check that your subscriptions are still attached to the version before you resubmit. I had to re-attach mine more than once.
  • Agreements aren't active. Until the Paid Apps Agreement, tax and banking are active, products may not load, so the reviewer sees an empty paywall.

Fix, step by step

  1. In App Store Connect, open each subscription and fill everything until it reads Ready to Submit: display name and description, price, availability, subscription group localization, the App Review screenshot of your paywall, and a review note.
  2. Check Business → Agreements: the Paid Apps Agreement must be active.
  3. Go to your app version page and refresh. In In-App Purchases and Subscriptions, click Edit and select every product.
  4. If the build is stuck in review, remove it from review, upload a new build if needed, re-link the products, and submit everything together.
  5. In the Resolution Center, write a short note, for example: "The subscriptions are now attached to this version and the Paid Apps Agreement is active."
  6. Using RevenueCat? Confirm the product IDs in App Store Connect match your RevenueCat products and the offering your app loads.

If a product is stuck in "Rejected" or "Developer Action Needed"

A rejected localization often can't be edited. Developers report it's sometimes faster to create a new product with a new product ID (deleted IDs can't be reused), update the ID in your code or RevenueCat, build again, and submit.
